So you stop hiding who the plan is for.

You film a fifteen-second video. Day one of your old notebook. Ten squats at the kitchen counter.

No music. No six-pack. Just you, out of breath, telling the truth.

You post one every day. Your actual old days, in order.

The views are tiny. Forty. Sixty. You keep going.

You almost stop on day eleven. Then a single comment appears. "Doing day one with you tomorrow."

Then on day nineteen, you post the stairs. All fourteen. Still a little out of breath at the top.

You wake up to two hundred thousand views.

The comments are not about muscles. They are about stairs.

People who have never felt welcome in a gym, finally seeing somebody who looks like them.

By the end of the week, the counter says four hundred and twelve.

Then the first hard message. "I did week one and my knees hurt. Is this even safe?"

You do not guess. You pay a physiotherapist to check every week of the plan.

You add easier versions of every move, and a page that says: if it hurts, stop, and ask a professional.

Because the people buying this are trusting you with their bodies. That is not something you rush.

You send the update free to every buyer, with a note saying sorry it was not there from the start.

That update gets you more five-star reviews than the original plan ever did.

People ask for more. So you add a walking plan. Then a cheap meal guide. Each one from a question in your comments.

Nine dollars at a time, the side project starts paying more than the call centre.

The credit card gets paid off first. Then the rent stops being a monthly panic.

And one morning, you hand back the headset.
