Then, at a routine check-up, the doctor goes quiet.

Your blood pressure is high. Not dangerous yet, she says. But heading there.

"You need to start moving. A little every day. Not someday. Now."

So you do what everybody does. You go to the big gym on the corner.

The manager is called Brick. He looks like he was built in a factory.

Sixty dollars a month. Twelve-month contract. Joining fee on top.

You ask if there is any way to pay a little less, just to start.

Brick laughs. "People like you sign up in January and quit in February. That's why we take the money upfront."

You walk out past a wall of mirrors. You do not look in any of them.

Your bank balance says three hundred and twelve dollars. Rent is due in nine days.

You cannot afford the gym. And according to your doctor, you cannot afford to wait.

And that is when you see it clearly. Your body and your bank account are stuck in the same place.

On the walk home, you pass a bus stop advert. A man with a perfect body, and two words. No excuses.

You have plenty of excuses. Every one of them is real. That is the problem.
