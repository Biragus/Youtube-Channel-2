You work in a call centre. Eight hours a day in a chair, with a headset on, for just above minimum wage.

It pays the rent. Most months. It does not pay for much else.

There is a credit card you try not to look at. Four thousand dollars, and the number only goes up.

Lunch comes from a vending machine. Dinner comes from whatever is open at ten at night.

You are not lazy. You are tired. There is a difference, and nobody seems to see it.

Somewhere along the way, the hoodie stopped being a style. It became a hiding place.

You stopped going to the beach. You stopped going in photos.

You tell yourself you will start on Monday. You have said that on a lot of Mondays.

You even bought running shoes once. They are still in the box, under the bed, with the price tag on.

At family dinner, your mom puts salad on your plate without a word.

Dex, who has been going to the gym since school, has advice.

"Bro, just eat less and move more. It's not that deep."

Everyone has a simple answer. Nobody has to live in your body.
