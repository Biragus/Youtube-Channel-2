Month three. The doctor checks your blood pressure again.

She looks at the number twice. "Whatever you're doing, keep doing it."

You have lost six kilos. Slowly. The way the doctor said it should happen.

Your hoodie is loose. You take it off at work for the first time in two years.

A coworker you have sat next to for two years says, "Have you done something different?" You nearly laugh.

Then Sam from the next desk asks you, quietly, how you did it.

He cannot afford the gym either. He works nights at a hospital as well. Two jobs, no time.

You photocopy your notebook for him. The whole plan, week by week.

Six weeks later, Sam sends you a message. "I did the stairs. All of them."

And then his sister asks for a copy. And then her friend.

And that is when you realise the notebook is not just yours anymore.

Something you made out of being broke and out of breath might be worth something to someone else.
