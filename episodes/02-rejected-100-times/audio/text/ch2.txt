You are twenty-seven. You have a degree, a student loan, and a name badge that says trainee.

You did everything they told you to do.

Study hard. Get the paper. The job will follow.

The job did not follow.

So you work the night shift, ten at night until six in the morning, putting tins on shelves.

The shop is so quiet at four in the morning that you can hear the fridges breathing.

You sleep in the same bedroom you had when you were twelve.

Every morning, your mom leaves a plate in the microwave, and a note that says, any news?

There is never any news.

The loan company sends a letter every month. The number on it only goes in one direction.

You stopped opening the envelopes in March.

At family dinner, Uncle Ray has a theory. "Nobody wants to work anymore."

You have applied for ninety-nine jobs.

Your friend Dex got hired in a week. His dad plays golf with the owner.

"Honestly, man. Just put yourself out there."

You have been out there for two years.

Two years of cover letters. Two years of writing, I am passionate about logistics. You are not passionate about logistics.

And the worst part is not the no.

It is the silence. Most of them never answer at all.
