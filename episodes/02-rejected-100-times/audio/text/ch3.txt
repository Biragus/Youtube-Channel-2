Then, finally, somebody calls you back.

You iron your only shirt. Twice.

You practise your answers in the bathroom mirror until three in the morning.

You even look up the name of the company's founder, in case it comes up. It does not come up.

The hiring manager is called Mr. Graves.

He looks at your résumé for four seconds. You count.

"Night shift. Supermarket. And you want to work here?"

"You are just not what we are looking for."

You thank him. You actually thank him.

You walk out past a wall of framed awards, and you feel about three inches tall.

On the bus home, the email arrives anyway. Number one hundred.

That night, you open a spreadsheet. You have logged every application. Every date. Every silence.

One hundred rows. All of them red.

And for the first time, you say the thing out loud. "Maybe it is me."
