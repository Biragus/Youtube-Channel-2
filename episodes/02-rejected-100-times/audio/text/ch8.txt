So you ask the question you asked before. What are they actually searching for?

Nobody is searching for a template. They are searching for an answer to the silence.

So you stop selling the kit. And you show the wall.

You post all one hundred rejections. Every one of them.

And underneath, one line. "Here is what I changed before number one hundred and one."

You post it at midnight, and you go to sleep.

You wake up to forty-one thousand views.

And one notification you have never seen before.

Twelve dollars. From a nurse, in a city you have never been to.

By lunchtime, there are thirty more.

You answer every single message. All of them. It takes until four in the morning, and for once, you do not mind being awake.

People send you their old résumés. You tear one apart on camera, kindly, every day.

Then comes the first angry message. "I bought your kit. I still got rejected."

You could delete it. Instead, you reply in public. "Send it to me. I will rewrite it myself. Free."

It takes you four hours.

Nine days later, she posts again. A photo of an office badge. With her name on it.

That post sells more kits than everything you ever wrote about yourself.

Because nobody trusts a stranger's success story. They trust a stranger's badge.

You add an interview guide. Then a salary script. Each one built from a question somebody asked.

Twelve dollars at a time, it stops looking like homework.

And one morning, you hand in a badge of your own.
