You are twenty-six years old, and you deliver parcels for a company that does not know your name.

To them, you are a number on a badge. Driver four four seven one.

Ten hours a day. Six days a week. Two hundred stops before the sun goes down.

Your van is older than your career, and it sounds like it knows it.

Every month ends the same way. The money runs out about a week before the month does.

You live in a room so small you can touch both walls at the same time.

There is a leak in the ceiling. You have named it. That is how long it has been there.

Maya left four months ago.

She did not shout. She just said she was tired of waiting for your life to start.

And the worst part is, you could not think of a single thing to say back.

Every Sunday, at dinner, Uncle Ray asks you the same question. "Still driving the van?"

Everybody laughs. You laugh too, because it is easier than not laughing.

Your mom squeezes your hand under the table. Somehow, that makes it worse.

At work, Mr. Graves times your bathroom breaks with a stopwatch.

"Four minutes, four four seven one. That is coming out of your lunch."

You tell yourself this is temporary. You have been telling yourself that for five years.

And every night, you fall asleep hoping that tomorrow, somehow, will be different.
