# Episode 01 — She Called Him "The Brokey". Then He Pulled Up.

- **Target length:** ~10 minutes (about 1,450 words of narration at a calm, deliberate pace, plus pauses and music-only beats)
- **Narration:** third person, present tense, one deep calm male voice
- **Characters:** Marcus (black stick figure, the hero), Nia (the ex), Mr. Okafor (first customer), Dev (first hire)
- **Format:** `[VISUAL]` lines describe what is on screen; everything else is spoken narration

---

## 1. Cold open (0:00 – 0:40)

[VISUAL: Night. A restaurant front. Nia and two friends stand outside laughing. Engine sound grows.]

A Friday night. A woman stands outside the most expensive restaurant in the city, laughing with her friends.

[VISUAL: An orange Lamborghini rolls into frame and stops. The friends go quiet.]

A car pulls up. The kind of car that makes a whole street go silent.

[VISUAL: The door lifts. A stick figure steps out. Nia's jaw drops.]

The door opens. And she whispers four words.

"Is that… the brokey?"

[VISUAL: Freeze frame. Screen drains to grey. Text: THREE YEARS EARLIER.]

To understand that moment, you have to go back. Three years. To the worst night of his life.

## 2. Rock bottom (0:40 – 2:00)

[VISUAL: Marcus in a warehouse, carrying boxes. A clock spins.]

His name is Marcus. He is twenty-four. He works ten hours a day in a warehouse, lifting boxes that belong to somebody else.

[VISUAL: Phone screen: BALANCE $11.42.]

He has eleven dollars and forty-two cents in his account. And tonight is his anniversary.

[VISUAL: Restaurant table. Marcus and Nia. A waiter holds a card machine. Red text: DECLINED.]

He takes Nia to dinner anyway. He tells himself the card will go through. It does not.

[VISUAL: Nia pays. Her face is flat.]

She pays. She does not say a word until they are outside.

[VISUAL: Street. Nia facing Marcus. Rain starts.]

Then she says it. "Marcus, I am twenty-three. I am not going to spend my life with a brokey."

[VISUAL: Nia walks away. Her friends at the corner laugh. Marcus stands alone in the rain.]

She walks away. Her friends are waiting on the corner. He can hear them laughing. He stands there in the rain, and he does not move for a long time.

## 3. The floor (2:00 – 3:00)

[VISUAL: Small dark bedroom. Marcus on the edge of the bed, phone glowing.]

That night he does what everybody does. He lies in the dark and scrolls.

[VISUAL: Phone: Nia in a photo beside a new guy and a shiny car.]

Two weeks later, she posts a photo. New guy. Nice car. Big smile.

[VISUAL: Marcus puts the phone face down. Silence.]

And here is the part nobody tells you. He does not jump up and swear revenge. There is no speech. He just feels small.

[VISUAL: He picks up a marker and writes "$0" on the wall.]

But somewhere around three in the morning, he takes a marker and writes a number on his wall. Zero dollars. That is what he has made on his own. Not for a boss. For himself.

He decides that number is going to change. He does not know how yet.

## 4. The first dollar (3:00 – 4:30)

[VISUAL: Warehouse car park. Dusty cars in rows. Marcus looks at them.]

The answer is sitting in the car park at his job. Two hundred cars. Every single one of them dirty.

[VISUAL: Shop shelf. Bucket, sponge, soap. Price tag $40. Balance drops to almost nothing.]

He spends forty dollars he does not really have. A bucket. Two sponges. Soap. A cheap bottle of wax.

[VISUAL: Marcus knocks on doors. Door after door slams. Counter: NO ×1 … NO ×31.]

On Saturday he knocks on doors. "Car wash, twenty dollars, I come to you." Thirty-one people say no. Some of them do not even open the door.

[VISUAL: Door 32. An old man, Mr. Okafor, squints at him. A filthy old sedan in the drive.]

Door thirty-two. An old man called Mr. Okafor. He looks at Marcus for a long second and says, "Son, that car has not been washed since my wife passed. Go ahead."

[VISUAL: Marcus washing in fast motion. The car begins to shine.]

Marcus spends three hours on a twenty-dollar job. He cleans that car like it is the only car on earth.

[VISUAL: Mr. Okafor hands him a $20 note. Bedroom wall: "$0" crossed out, "$20" written.]

The old man hands him a twenty. That night, Marcus crosses out the zero.

Twenty dollars. It is nothing. And it is everything. Because it is the first money he ever made that nobody could take away by firing him.

## 5. The grind (4:30 – 6:10)

[VISUAL: Alarm clock 5:00. Marcus washing cars at sunrise, then warehouse, then laptop at night. Loop speeds up.]

So he builds a routine. Up at five. Two cars before his shift. Ten hours in the warehouse. Then home, watching detailing videos until his eyes close.

[VISUAL: Wall counter climbs: $20 → $340 → $1,150.]

No weekends. No nights out. Every dollar goes back in. Better polish. A pressure washer. Business cards with his name on them.

[VISUAL: Mr. Okafor talking to neighbours, pointing at his gleaming car. Phone buzzing.]

Mr. Okafor tells his whole street. The phone starts ringing.

[VISUAL: Marcus polishing a black BMW. A long scratch appears. Red flash.]

And then he makes a mistake. A rushed job on a black BMW. He leaves a scratch across the entire door.

[VISUAL: Invoice: REPAIR $600. Wall counter drops.]

Six hundred dollars to fix. Almost everything he has saved. That night he sits on the floor and thinks, maybe she was right. Maybe I am just a brokey with a bucket.

[VISUAL: Marcus knocks on the BMW owner's door, hands over an envelope.]

But in the morning he drives to the owner's house. He tells the truth. He pays for every cent of the repair. And he says, "Let me do your next three cars for free."

[VISUAL: The owner shakes his hand. Sign behind him: car dealership.]

That man owns a car dealership.

Here is the lesson, and it is worth more than the six hundred dollars. People do not remember that you made a mistake. They remember what you did next.

## 6. Leverage (6:10 – 7:40)

[VISUAL: Dealership lot, forty cars. Contract paper: MONTHLY.]

The dealership needs forty cars cleaned every single week. Marcus cannot do forty cars alone.

[VISUAL: Marcus hands in his warehouse badge and walks out into sunlight.]

So he does the scariest thing he has ever done. He quits the warehouse.

[VISUAL: Marcus and Dev shake hands. A used van with a logo: MARCUS MOBILE DETAIL.]

He hires his first employee. A kid named Dev, from the same warehouse. He buys a used van with two hundred thousand miles on it.

[VISUAL: One van becomes three, then twelve. Map with pins spreading.]

And this is where it changes. For a year, Marcus traded his hours for money. Now he builds a system. One van becomes three. Three become twelve.

[VISUAL: Chart line climbing. Wall counter: $9,000 / month → $80,000 / month.]

He stops washing cars and starts training the people who wash them. He writes down every step. He answers every complaint himself.

[VISUAL: A row of supercars outside a glass building. Marcus's van parked beside them.]

Then the luxury owners call. People with garages worth more than his whole neighbourhood. They do not want the cheapest. They want the one who tells the truth when he scratches a door.

## 7. The empire (7:40 – 8:40)

[VISUAL: A large building. Sign going up: MARCUS AUTO CARE. Staff lined up outside.]

Three years after the worst night of his life, Marcus owns four locations and employs sixty-one people.

[VISUAL: Mr. Okafor cuts a ribbon. Marcus beside him.]

Mr. Okafor cuts the ribbon at the newest one. His car is still washed for free. It always will be.

[VISUAL: Bedroom wall, now framed in an office: "$0" crossed out at the top of a long list of numbers.]

The wall from his old bedroom hangs in his office. The crossed-out zero is still at the top.

[VISUAL: Showroom. An orange Lamborghini. Marcus looks at it, then at his own reflection in the paint.]

And one afternoon, he walks into a showroom. Not to clean the cars. He has cleaned hundreds of these. He knows every curve.

This time, he buys one.

## 8. The return (8:40 – 9:35)

[VISUAL: Back to the cold open. Colour returns. The Lamborghini door lifts. Marcus steps out.]

Which brings us back to Friday night.

"Is that… the brokey?"

[VISUAL: Nia stares. Her friends stare at her. Marcus nods politely and hands the keys to the valet.]

Marcus sees her. He nods. He does not gloat. He does not say a word. He just walks inside, where his team is waiting to celebrate.

[VISUAL: Phone buzz. Message from NIA: "I always knew you'd make it. Dinner sometime? ❤️"]

An hour later, his phone lights up. "I always knew you would make it. Dinner sometime?"

[VISUAL: Marcus looks at the phone. Looks at the table of laughing employees, Dev, Mr. Okafor.]

He looks at the message. Then he looks around the table. At Dev. At Mr. Okafor. At the people who were there when he had a bucket and a sponge.

[VISUAL: He types: "I hope you're doing well, Nia. Take care." Phone goes face down.]

He writes back, "I hope you are doing well. Take care." And he puts the phone face down.

## 9. The lesson (9:35 – 10:05)

[VISUAL: Marcus alone beside the car at night, city lights behind.]

Because here is the truth. The car was never the point. She was never the point.

[VISUAL: Split screen: Marcus in the rain, Marcus now.]

The point is the man in the rain, with eleven dollars, who decided that zero was not going to be his number.

[VISUAL: A bucket and sponge on a doorstep. Sunrise.]

You do not need a big idea. You need a bucket, thirty-one no's, and the nerve to knock on door thirty-two.

[VISUAL: Text on screen: WHAT IS YOUR DOOR 32?]

So. What is your door thirty-two?
